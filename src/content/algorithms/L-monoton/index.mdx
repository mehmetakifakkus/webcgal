## What is being monotone for a polygon?

A simple polygon is called *monotone with respect to a line l* if for any line *l'* perpendicular to *l* the intersection of the polygon with *l'* is connected. In other words, the intersection should be a line segment, a point, or empty. A polygon that is monotone with respect to the y-axis is called *y-monotone*. The following property is characteristic for y-monotone polygons: if we walk from a topmost to a bottommost vertex along the left (or the right) boundary chain, then we always move downwards or horizontally, never upwards.

This application shows the available line orientations which make a polygon monotone. In the left canvas you can modify the position of the polygon points (drag a vertex or the whole polygon; Ctrl+click adds a point). Points which make the polygon concave appear colored, the others are black.

You will immediately see the resulting line orientation in the right canvas. Its upper figure shows the inner angle of the concave points and the lower one their intersection.

For more information you can refer to [wiki/Monotone_polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_polygon).
