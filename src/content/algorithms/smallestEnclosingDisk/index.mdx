Let *P* be a set of *n* points in the plane. The *smallest enclosing disc* of *P* is the disc of smallest radius that contains every point of *P*; its centre is the point whose largest distance to the points is as small as possible, for example the best place for a facility that has to serve all of them. This disc is unique: if two different discs of the same smallest radius both contained *P*, then *P* would lie in their intersection, and that lens fits in a disc of smaller radius centred midway between the two centres. For two or more points the disc has at least two of them on its boundary, and it is determined by two of them (as the endpoints of a diameter) or by three.

This page follows section 4.7, *Smallest Enclosing Discs*, of Computational Geometry: Algorithms and Applications, 3rd ed., by de Berg et al.

**The incremental idea.** Put the points in a random order *p<sub>1</sub>*, …, *p<sub>n</sub>* and let *D<sub>i</sub>* be the smallest enclosing disc of the first *i* of them. *D<sub>2</sub>* is the disc with *p<sub>1</sub>p<sub>2</sub>* as a diameter. When *p<sub>i</sub>* is added, either it lies in *D<sub>i−1</sub>*, and then *D<sub>i</sub>* = *D<sub>i−1</sub>*, or it lies outside, and then *p<sub>i</sub>* lies on the boundary of the new disc *D<sub>i</sub>*. This is Lemma 4.14 of the book, stated for discs that must also have a given set *R* of points on their boundary: if some disc encloses *P* with all of *R* on its boundary, the smallest such disc is unique; adding a point that lies in it changes nothing, and adding a point outside it gives the smallest disc with that point added to *R*.

**Why three procedures.** A point that falls outside does not make the work start over from nothing: it becomes a *fixed* boundary point, and the remaining question is the same problem with one more condition. **MiniDisc** adds the points one by one; for a point *q* outside its disc it calls **MiniDiscWithPoint**, which adds *p<sub>1</sub>*, …, *p<sub>i−1</sub>* again, now to discs that have *q* on their boundary, starting with the disc with *q* and *p<sub>1</sub>* as a diameter. A point outside one of those discs becomes the second fixed point, and **MiniDiscWith2Points** repeats the scan with *q<sub>1</sub>* and *q<sub>2</sub>* on the boundary. There a point outside is the third boundary point, and three points determine a disc, the circle through them, so no fourth level is needed: the disc is updated directly.

**Expected linear time.** MiniDiscWith2Points takes O(*n*) time for *n* points. In MiniDiscWithPoint, the *j*-th point costs more than constant time only when it lies outside *D<sub>j−1</sub>*, and then the call to MiniDiscWith2Points costs O(*j*). *Backwards analysis* bounds how often this happens: fix the set of the first *j* points, so that only the last of them, *p<sub>j</sub>*, is random. *D<sub>j</sub>* is determined by at most three points on its boundary, one of which is *q*, and removing *p<sub>j</sub>* changes the disc only if *p<sub>j</sub>* is one of the other two, which has probability at most 2/*j*. The expected time is therefore O(*n*) plus the sum of O(*j*) · 2/*j* over all *j*, which is O(*n*). MiniDisc has no fixed point, so the probability that *p<sub>i</sub>* lies outside *D<sub>i−1</sub>* is at most 3/*i*, and the whole algorithm runs in O(*n*) expected time with linear storage. The bound only needs the order to be random, not the points: in a bad order every level can call the next one at nearly every step, and the three nested loops then only give O(*n*<sup>3</sup>). The *Bad order* preset places ten points on a spiral from the centre outwards and keeps them in that order, so nearly every point falls outside the current disc: it ends after 110 tests and 41 updates, against 17 tests and 4 updates for the ten points of the *Random example*.

Step through the algorithm with the player. The **Call stack** panel shows the procedures that are running, with the loop counter (*i*, *j* or *k*) of each; **Order** shows the random order, with the point under test and the fixed points of the innermost call highlighted; **Disc** shows the centre and radius of the current disc and the number of tests and updates so far. On the canvas the point under test turns green when it lies inside the disc and red when it lies outside, the fixed points are ringed, and the discs of the calling procedures are drawn thin and dashed. The presets include two far-apart points, an acute triangle whose three corners define the disc, points on a circle and collinear points. Drag the points to change the input; the order stays. **New random input** draws new points and a new random order.
